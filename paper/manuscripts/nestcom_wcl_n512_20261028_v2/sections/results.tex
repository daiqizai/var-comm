\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We use common500 ImageNet validation images~\cite{imagenet}, a disjoint 1000-image calibration set, and the 24 Kodak images~\cite{kodak}, with fixed $256\times256$ preprocessing. The six-system main experiment uses $N=1024$; the reduced-budget study compares NeST, WHOLE, and Ada at $N=512$. Both use $E_s=2$ and 4/10/19~dB. ImageNet-calibrated policies remain fixed on Kodak.

The six systems are NeST-Com, Latent JSCC, adapted SwinJSCC~\cite{swinjscc}, adaptive BPG~\cite{bpg} + LDPC, adapted ARPC~\cite{arpc} + LDPC, and Ada-TokenCom adapted to a fixed per-image frame. Our convolutional Latent JSCC maps tokenizer latents to symbols and shares $\Dc$; its losses combine image MSE, LPIPS, and normalized-latent consistency. Latent JSCC and SwinJSCC use the same 20\,000 training images and final 100k/200k-update checkpoints. SwinJSCC was still improving at its training budget cap. All required signaling is charged; ARPC's caption is paid. Ada retains LlamaGen-L/VQ16, prefix arithmetic coding, and conditional tail sampling. A fixed ConvNeXt predicts the class, whose 10-bit ID is transmitted, giving $b+39\leq k_a$. Calibration chooses its MCS; online fitting searches the predeclared 1--256-token endpoints. This changes Ada's average-resource formulation to our fixed-frame task.

Metrics are PSNR, LPIPS-Alex~\cite{lpips}, DreamSim~\cite{dreamsim}, and CLIP-I, the CLIP ViT-L/14 image--image cosine~\cite{clip}. DreamSim/CLIP use frozen FP32, $224\times224$ preprocessing. We average three channel realizations per source, retaining gray failures, then compute equal-source means. Intervals are 95\% source-bootstrap CIs; differences are directly source-paired. Pairing does not require identical noise realizations across methods. Generative seeds and decoding settings are fixed. N512 methods share source/repeat-specific AWGN; the historical N1024 systems retain their original noise realizations. At $N=1024$, no rejected or failed reconstructions occurred in the 1500 trials per method at each matched SNR on common500. Continuous Latent JSCC has no discrete CRC rejection rule. Transmitter fallback is counted separately.

Table~\ref{tab:internal}(A) compares WHOLE/PARTIAL with shared tokenizer, prior, entropy coder, and $\Dc$. Their target/MCS choices are calibrated separately and select the same MCS at each SNR; fallback follows the fixed rule. On common500, PSNR gains are 0.32--0.70~dB. At 10~dB, utilization rises from 77.70\% to 95.21\% and DreamSim decreases by 0.0240. Kodak gains 0.674~dB and reduces DreamSim by 0.0164 at 10~dB; its 19~dB DreamSim CI includes zero. These results connect additional legal endpoints and calibrated fitting to better use of a fixed body capacity.

Block~B isolates granularity on 100 existing sources, holding model, MCS, maximum endpoint, and longest-feasible fitting fixed. WHOLE, half-, quarter-, and eighth-scale grids have 7/13/25/49 candidates. At 10~dB, QUARTER minus WHOLE gains $0.660\,[0.515,0.814]$~dB, while EIGHTH adds $0.123\,[0.079,0.169]$~dB over QUARTER. The denser grid also yields small PSNR/LPIPS gains at other SNRs; the main quarter grid remains the predefined configuration.

A receiver intervention uses the same 100 sources and actual received prefixes, without new endpoint selection or channel trials. A0 ignores received boundary positions and follows the prior path. B0 adds their correction only to the final residual latent, leaving finer-scale context on the A0 path. C0 updates both latent and context. All three use the same device and numerical precision. At 10~dB, B0$-$A0 and C0$-$B0 yield 0.220 and 0.440~dB mean PSNR differences. C0$-$B0 is zero at 19~dB because the boundary is the last scale.

\subsection{Comparison With Existing Methods}
\input{tables/table_ii}
Fig.~\ref{fig:performance} and Table~\ref{tab:external} show a quality tradeoff. NeST-Com exceeds adapted Ada in PSNR by 4.116/0.455/1.226~dB at 4/10/19~dB, respectively. At 10~dB, Ada has lower LPIPS and DreamSim and higher CLIP-I, with source-paired CIs excluding zero. Ada also has higher CLIP-I at 4~dB and slightly lower DreamSim at 19~dB; NeST has better PSNR, LPIPS, and CLIP-I at 19~dB. Ada delivers all 256 tokens at 19~dB, so no tail sampling occurs; its approximately 58\% source-capacity utilization reflects completion of its representation.

SwinJSCC and BPG have higher PSNR than NeST at every tested SNR. Latent JSCC has higher PSNR at 4/10~dB and lower LPIPS at 4~dB. Thus perceptual criteria and pixel error need not rank systems alike. NeST's PSNR advantage over ARPC is 3.26--3.97~dB. On matched100, replacing ARPC's original $\tau=0.5$ sampling by argmax or $\tau=0.25$ changes mean PSNR by up to about 0.15~dB in the tested conditions.

Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows two selected development examples. Ada retains crisp pastry folds and dog fur; NeST and Latent JSCC smooth different details, while ARPC changes visible object contours and texture. For the dog, Latent JSCC has the lowest displayed LPIPS, whereas Ada has the lowest DreamSim. These individual outcomes complement the source-level comparison.

On Kodak, all four paired metrics favor NeST over Ada at 4~dB. At 10~dB, only the PSNR difference has a 95\% paired confidence interval excluding zero; at 19~dB, PSNR and LPIPS favor NeST while DreamSim/CLIP-I CIs cross zero.

\subsection{Transmission With a Reduced Symbol Budget}
\input{tables/table_iii}
At $N=512$, 68 header symbols leave 444 body symbols. Policies are independently recalibrated on the calibration set using a common MCS pool. WHOLE achieves 3.79 and 3.66~dB higher mean PSNR than the fixed-frame Ada-TokenCom adaptation at 4 and 10~dB, respectively: a source-reconstruction advantage is already present with whole-scale endpoints. Within-scale transmission further improves PSNR over WHOLE by 0.39--0.79~dB across the three SNRs. It also improves LPIPS, DreamSim, and CLIP-I, with all source-paired 95\% CIs excluding zero. At 10~dB, WHOLE and NeST-Com select the same target $(7,0)$ and 16-QAM rate-2/3 MCS; within-scale fallback endpoints raise source-bit utilization from 79.30\% to 93.46\%.

Compared with Ada, NeST-Com has higher PSNR and lower LPIPS at every tested SNR, and lower DreamSim at 4/10~dB (Table~\ref{tab:reduced}). Ada has higher CLIP-I throughout and lower DreamSim at 19~dB; these paired CIs exclude zero. The results support the next-scale representation and reconstruction system under a tight budget, with further gains from within-scale transmission. Cross-budget comparisons describe separately calibrated systems, whose candidate pools differ; they do not isolate representation order or a change of $N$ alone. Body-CRC rejections are 4/7/5 for NeST/WHOLE/Ada at 4~dB, 1/0/0 at 10~dB, and zero at 19~dB, each out of 1500 trials. All remain in the means; no source requires NULL.

\subsection{Additional Results and Processing Cost}
The existing $N=2048$ comparison uses 100 sources and two paid 1024-symbol subframes, with adaptive conditions at 4/10~dB and complete representations at 19~dB. At 19~dB, NeST exceeds ARPC by 1.806~dB PSNR, but ARPC has better DISTS~\cite{dists}: NeST minus ARPC is $+0.0054\,[+0.0024,+0.0086]$.

With WHOLE/PARTIAL transmit policies fixed at 10~dB and RX using the true noise variance, all 300 receptions per method on a 100-image subset are rejected at 8~dB, whereas all succeed at 10/12~dB. For each source and method, the recovered prefix and reconstruction are identical at 10 and 12~dB; gray failures remain in the statistics.

Software processing latency at 10~dB averages 619.71~ms/image for NeST, 42.48 for Latent JSCC, 44.69 for SwinJSCC, 1520.30 for ARPC, and 2293.01 for BPG (RTX PRO~6000, six CPU cores, batch one, 16 development images measured three times after warm-up). Totals sum TX/RX software processing; Ada has not been timed under this protocol.

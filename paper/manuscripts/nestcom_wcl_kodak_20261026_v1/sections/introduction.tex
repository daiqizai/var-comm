\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{mage} communication with a fixed number of channel uses requires the source representation, signaling, and channel protection to share a finite budget. A generative receiver can reconstruct an image from an incomplete representation, making the choice of transmitted information central to reconstruction quality. Next-scale visual autoregressive (VAR) models~\cite{var} provide a useful representation for this setting: coarse token maps describe large structures, and successive scales refine their residual information. However, the number of tokens grows with scale, so stopping only after a complete scale can leave substantial capacity unused.

Deep joint source--channel coding (DeepJSCC) maps images directly to channel symbols~\cite{deepjscc}, and SwinJSCC uses hierarchical transformers to improve this mapping~\cite{swinjscc}. Generative image communication, including DiffCom~\cite{diffcom}, uses a learned prior to reconstruct images from channel observations. Discrete-token methods provide another route: Ada-TokenCom combines prefix transmission, arithmetic coding, tail generation, and rate/MCS adaptation~\cite{adatokencom}, while TONIC studies token protection and completion for task-oriented communication~\cite{tonic}. Closest to next-scale transmission, ARPC combines scale selection, autoregressive entropy coding, and generation of missing scales for image compression~\cite{arpc}. Its transmitted prefixes end at complete scales, and its compression formulation does not specify a fixed-symbol digital frame.

Progressive image transmission using embedded source codes and channel protection under a target transmission rate is a classical problem~\cite{chande}. For next-scale representations, a remaining issue is the granularity of legal token endpoints under a hard frame budget. Actual coded length varies across images, while complete-scale endpoints offer only coarse adjustments. We propose NeST-Com, which allows the transmitted prefix to end within a scale and fits its terminated arithmetic stream to the available frame capacity. The receiver reconstructs the coding probabilities from decoded coarse scales, preserves the received boundary tokens, and completes the remaining representation. Our same-prior comparison raises source-bit utilization from 77.70\% to 95.21\% at 10~dB and improves PSNR by 0.32--0.70~dB. On 500 ImageNet images at $N=1024$, NeST-Com achieves the lowest mean DreamSim and highest mean CLIP-I among five tested schemes at all three SNRs.

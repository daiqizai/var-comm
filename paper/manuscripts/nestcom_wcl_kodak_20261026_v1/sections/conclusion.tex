\section{Conclusion}
We presented NeST-Com, which fits next-scale token prefixes to a fixed-symbol digital frame using within-scale endpoints and actual terminated lengths. A shared prior supports entropy decoding and reconstruction constrained by received tokens. On common500, with the same prior and decoder, finer endpoints improve PSNR by 0.32--0.70~dB and increase utilization from approximately 78\% to 95\% at 10~dB. The five-method comparison shows favorable DreamSim and CLIP-I performance, together with SNR-dependent fidelity tradeoffs and higher processing cost than continuous JSCC. Future work will extend endpoint and MCS selection to fading channels and uncertain SNR estimates.

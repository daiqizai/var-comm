\section{Conclusion}
NeST-Com pairs a frame-fitting rule with a receiver that uses the recovered boundary tokens. A coder-specific nondecreasing-length property allows the transmitter to select the longest independently terminated prefix that fits the frame. At the receiver, the recovered tokens overwrite boundary predictions, correcting residuals and updating the context for finer-scale prediction. On 500 ImageNet images at $N=1024$ and 10 dB, NeST-Com raises source-bit capacity utilization from 77.70\% to 99.79\% and reduces DISTS and DreamSim by 8.7\% and 20.5\% relative to the shared-model whole-scale baseline. The Eighth grid offers lower transmitter cost with a small quality loss relative to Token. This tradeoff suits image transmission under tight symbol budgets when the application prioritizes perceptual quality and can accommodate the measured processing time.

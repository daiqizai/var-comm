\begin{abstract}
Next-scale token maps provide a coarse-to-fine source description, but whole-scale endpoints can underfill fixed digital frames. NeST-Com retains scale-wise prediction while allowing transmission to stop within a scale. It fits independently terminated prefixes using their actual lengths and incorporates received boundary tokens into the residual state for subsequent prediction. At 1024 symbols and 10 dB, source-bit utilization increases from 77.70\% to 99.79\%, reducing DISTS and DreamSim by 8.7\% and 20.5\% relative to shared-model whole-scale transmission. Matched controls establish the granularity effect, while different budgets and measured processing costs reveal quality--cost tradeoffs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A fixed per-image channel-symbol budget must accommodate source information, signaling, and channel protection. Digital coding and learned joint source--channel coding (JSCC) address this constraint through different representations~\cite{deepjscc,swinjscc}. With a generative receiver, delivered information constrains reconstruction to the source image while a learned prior predicts untransmitted content. Efficient communication depends on both retaining useful information within the frame and using it during reconstruction.

Next-scale tokenization organizes an image into complete coarse maps followed by residual refinements. Each resized coarse map covers the full latent grid, providing a basis for finer-scale prediction. Visual autoregressive modeling (VAR) reported state-of-the-art class-conditional ImageNet generation results~\cite{var}, motivating its use as a completion prior. ARPC uses next-scale entropy coding and scale completion for progressive image compression~\cite{arpc}. Ada-TokenCom couples next-token prefix coding and tail generation with wireless resource adaptation~\cite{adatokencom}; TONIC studies task-oriented token transmission~\cite{tonic}. We develop next-scale image communication under a hard symbol budget for each image.

Adapting progressive descriptions to channel resources is a classical communication problem~\cite{chande}. Next-scale boundaries can leave usable frame capacity: a complete map may not fit although a subset of its tokens would. Entropy-code lengths vary across images, so fitting requires actual decodable lengths. The resulting incomplete boundary map must then contribute to residual reconstruction and to the conditions for predicting finer scales.

NeST-Com addresses these requirements through within-scale endpoints, actual-length fitting, and prefix-constrained reconstruction. Both terminals obtain boundary coding probabilities from complete coarse maps. Independently finalized arithmetic prefixes provide decodable candidates; received tokens overwrite boundary predictions~\cite{var}, and the merged map updates residual accumulation and finer-scale conditioning. Shared-model experiments show lower LPIPS and DreamSim than whole-scale transmission at all six matched budget/SNR points. At 1024 symbols and 10~dB, source-bit utilization rises from 77.70\% to 95.21\%, with LPIPS and DreamSim reductions of 13.7\% and 18.3\%. Endpoint controls and receiver interventions explain these gains; system comparisons include digital adaptations of ARPC and Ada-TokenCom under the same frame budget.

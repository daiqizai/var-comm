**Fig. 2.** Five-method comparison on common500 with a budget of N = 1024 complex channel uses per image over AWGN. Each source is averaged over three noise realizations before equally weighting the 500 sources. Points and error bars show the frozen means and pointwise 95% confidence intervals. NeST-Com uses the entropy-coded partial-scale interface; ARPC uses the adapted LDPC–QAM interface. Latent JSCC and SwinJSCC use their final 100k and 200k checkpoints. Prediction agreement is the fraction of images whose ConvNeXt top-1 prediction matches that of the source image. The 19 dB NeST-Com results include the T1-C m10 correction. Historical noise realizations differ across methods.

**Fig. 3.** Partial-scale versus whole-scale-only source coding on common500 at N = 1024. (a) PSNR gain of NeST-Com over the whole-scale-only variant, with direct source-paired 95% confidence intervals after averaging three noise realizations per source; the dashed line marks zero gain. (b) Mean source-capacity utilization: terminated source-coded payload length divided by the available source-bit capacity. Bars show source means, with numerical labels rounded only for display; no uncertainty interval is inferred from the utilization percentiles. Both families use the corrected m10 results at 19 dB.

**Table I.** Source-paired NeST–ARPC differences at N = 2048 on matched100. Each source has three noise realizations, and both methods correctly deliver 300/300 frames in each reported condition. Values are mean differences with direct source-paired 95% confidence intervals, using Δ = NeST − ARPC. Positive ΔPSNR favors NeST; positive ΔDISTS favors ARPC. The adaptive 4/10 dB results and full-representation 19 dB results use different frozen configurations.
